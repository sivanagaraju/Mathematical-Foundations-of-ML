# References — Lecture 16: Pedagogy in the Times of AI

## Primary Literature & Theoretical Notes
- **Geoffrey Hinton (2024):** *"Nobel Lecture & The Economic Consequences of AI Abundance."* Nobel Prize in Physics 2024.  
  [https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/hinton/facts/](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/hinton/facts/)  
  *Why Read This:* Directly discussed in Lecture 16. Hinton argues that intelligence commoditization under capitalist incentive structures breeds massive wealth and power concentration rather than universal societal flourishing.
- **John Sweller (1988):** *"Cognitive Load Theory: Schema Acquisition and the Limitations of Working Memory."* Cognition and Instruction, 12(4), 257-285.  
  [https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1204_3](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1204_3)  
  *Why Read This:* Foundational psychological paper explaining why passive reading and pre-chewed AI answers fail to build long-term memory schemas.
- **Benjamin Bloom et al. (1956, Revised by Anderson & Krathwohl 2001):** *"A Taxonomy for Learning, Teaching, and Assessing."* Longman.  
  [https://www.jstor.org/stable/1477405](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1477405)  
  *Why Read This:* Establishes the cognitive hierarchy (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create) that modern generative AI has inverted.
- **Plato (approx. 380 BCE):** *"The Republic (The Allegory of the Cave & Socratic Dialectic)."* Translated by Benjamin Jowett.  
  [https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1497](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1497)  
  *Why Read This:* Foundational philosophical text on the Socratic method, embodying the dialectical tension between shadows of knowledge and mathematical enlightenment.

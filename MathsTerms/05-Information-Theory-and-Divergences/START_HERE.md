# Start Here: A First-Principles Route into Information Theory & Divergences for AI

> **Who this is for:** A developer or researcher who wants to master Shannon entropy, cross-entropy, Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, Jensen-Shannon divergence, general $f$-divergence families, and Wasserstein optimal transport without getting lost in measure-theoretic abstractions or hand-waving proofs.
>
> **What this page does:** It gives a recommended route into information theory and statistical divergences. The first four chapters form one dependency chain. Variational divergence minimization and Wasserstein distance then form two later branches with different prerequisites.

---

## The honest starting point

To begin the entropy chapter, you need basic discrete probability and logarithms. Later chapters introduce additional requirements when they first become necessary: expectations and likelihood for KL, convexity and Fenchel conjugacy for $f$-divergence and VDM, and norms plus Lipschitz continuity for Wasserstein distance.

You do **not** need measure-theoretic differential geometry or Borel probability metrics to begin. Every divergence, informational quantity, and transport cost is grounded with intuitive physical analogies, spoken pronunciations, and pencil-and-paper micro-numerical examples.

## How to use each chapter

1. Read each chapter's opening and follow its stated first-reading route; section numbers differ because each concept needs a different explanation.
2. Follow the main example from the motivating question through the definition and hand calculation before opening later applications.
3. Read unfamiliar notation aloud at first use and use the compact spoken-math review for revision.
4. Compare the result with the nearest alternative and state when each approach works.
5. Complete the closed-notes recall and transfer problem before opening the answer key.
6. Run code only after completing the hand calculation, then check that both produce the same result.

## What these concepts unlock in modern AI

- **Autoregressive Large Language Models (LLMs):** Cross-entropy next-token prediction and forward KL divergence minimization against the empirical data distribution.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) & RLHF:** Reverse KL divergence penalty constraints $\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta \parallel \pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$ preventing policy collapse during alignment.
- **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs):** Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) with analytical Gaussian KL divergence regularization $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma) \parallel \mathcal{N}(0, I))$.
- **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs & WGAN-GP):** Jensen-Shannon divergence in standard GANs, variational $f$-divergence estimation in $f$-GANs, and Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual optimal transport in Wasserstein GANs.
- **Diffusion Models:** Reverse-time score matching as equivalent to minimizing Fisher divergence and relative entropy along the diffusion trajectory.
